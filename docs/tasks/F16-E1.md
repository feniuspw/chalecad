## What to do
1. **Effective layout** (`layout`):
   - if `electrical.manual`, use the saved points, conduits, and circuits;
   - otherwise, **generate**: points by the minimums (reuse `points.ts` and `circuits.ts`):
     - ceiling light per room, in a grid by the lumen method with the default luminaire;
     - switch next to the door, 0.15 m from the jamb and at 1.10 m height, with `controls`; on stairs and long hallways, parallel (`two_way`);
     - outlets along the perimeter, on wall faces; 2 over the kitchen counter;
     - TUE (dedicated-use outlet) for the shower and for furniture appliances (`applianceByFurniture`);
     - circuits by the rules: lighting separate from outlets, kitchen and service exclusive, TUE above 10 A isolated, current limit per circuit, proximity;
     - conduits through the **ceiling cavity**: tree from the board to the points in orthogonal runs, drops down the wall to the boxes.
   - Generated ids with the `auto-` prefix, deterministic.
   - The generated layout must be a valid `Electrical`, because the interface copies this `layout` into the project when "editing by hand".
2. **Calculation** (all pure):
   - room of each point;
   - load per point (rule, appliance, or luminaires);
   - conduit graph: nodes at the endpoints, at the points, and at the board; connects endpoints coinciding within 2 cm, a point within ≤ 0.3 m of the polyline, and a vertical drop down to the box;
   - path of each circuit from the board to each point, and conductors per segment;
   - FCA (grouping correction factor) from the segment with the most circuits and FCT (temperature correction factor) from the temperature;
   - Ib → breaker (C curve; B for shower) → cross-section by the corrected capacity (minimum 1.5 mm² for lighting and 2.5 mm² for power) → voltage drop summed along the path, with resistance at 70 °C, and increase the cross-section if it exceeds 4% → PE equal to the phase;
   - RCD (DR) per wet room (lights at 2.5 m or higher are left out), grouped in up to 5 circuits per RCD;
   - SPD (DPS) by the voltage;
   - board: modules, spares, commercial size, and main breaker;
   - demand by the table and the probable service entrance category;
   - feeder (total drop ≤ 5%);
   - fill of each conduit and minimum DN;
   - box rule: 15 m − 3 m per bend, at most 3 bends;
   - lighting design per room: K = c·l/(h·(c+l)) using the bounding rectangle, Fu (utilization factor) by interpolation, E = Φ·Fu·Fm/A and status against the targets.
   - User overrides (`CircuitDef.sectionMm2`, `breakerA`, `curve`, `dr`) are respected and verified.
3. **Warnings** with `elec.*` codes from table 8.4: the ones that make sense with this data, with `refs` to highlight on the floor plan. Severity: `error` only for clear code items; the rest `warn` or `info`.
4. **Budget items** (stage `installations`): cables by cross-section (m, with allowance and loss), conduit by DN, boxes, modules, breakers, RCD, SPD, board, and luminaires, with prices in the pricebook. When the new calculation runs, the old estimated electrical items go away (no double counting).
5. **Performance:** `computeAll` with installations on a typical chalet must stay below about 15 ms (measure in a test with margin).

## Tests
- Those in section 8.7 of the research, with tolerances:
  - lighting 6 / 9.9 / 10 / 14 m²;
  - kitchen outlets 3 × 3.5 m;
  - 5,500 W shower at 220 V → 4 mm² and 25 A, drop of 2.69% over 20 m;
  - the same shower at 127 V → 10 mm² and warning `elec.shower.127v`;
  - 2,200 VA outlets with FCA 0.65 and a 16 A breaker → `elec.circuit.overload`;
  - fill of 3 circuits of 2.5 mm² → DN 25;
  - 4 × 5 living room with 4 × 1,800 lm → about 158 lux.
- Generated layout from a ready-made model: every room has a light and a switch, all points are in some circuit, all points are connected to the board by conduit, and the `layout` passes `Electrical.parse`.
- Manual: moving an outlet far away increases the circuit's length and drop.
- `pnpm --filter @chalecad/engine test` and `pnpm typecheck` green.
